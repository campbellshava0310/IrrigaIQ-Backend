## What a flow meter can and cannot tell you

A water meter records how much water moved, not why. An unusual reading is a signal to investigate,
not a diagnosis: the same pattern can come from a leak, a valve left open, a controller fault, an
irrigation someone ran without recording it, or a faulty meter. Conclusions should be worded as
possibilities until someone has looked.

## Flow outside scheduled irrigation

When no irrigation is scheduled, flow on an irrigation line should be close to zero. Water utilities
use the same idea as "minimum night flow": the lowest flow of the night reveals background leakage.
Sustained flow outside irrigation windows most often means a valve that did not close (a stuck
solenoid or a manual valve left open), a burst or leaking pipe, or an unrecorded irrigation.

## Patterns and their likely causes

A sudden, large and sustained increase suggests a burst pipe, a disconnected lateral or a valve
opened in error. A smaller increase that persists for days suggests a slow leak, a damaged lateral
or leaking fittings. Flow well below normal during a scheduled irrigation suggests a blockage, a
clogged filter, a closed valve, pump trouble or a falling water source. Flow that repeats at the same
unusual time every day suggests a controller programme error.

## How to investigate

First, check whether anyone ran an unrecorded irrigation or changed the controller programme. Then
check the valves on the affected line and the controller's status. Walk the line looking for wet
patches, pooled water, unusually green strips or eroded soil. If nothing is visible, close sections
one at a time while watching the meter: the section whose closure stops the flow contains the leak.
Finally, confirm the meter itself by comparing it with a known volume or a second meter.

## Why it matters

Beyond the cost of the water and the pumping energy, a leak can waterlog part of a field, leach
nutrients, and leave the rest of the block under-irrigated because pressure drops. Unexplained
consumption also counts against licensed water allocations.
