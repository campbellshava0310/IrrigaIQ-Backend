## How mulch saves water

Part of a crop's water use is evaporation from the wet soil surface, which is largest early in the
season when the canopy is small and after every irrigation or rain. Mulch shades and covers the soil
surface and so cuts this evaporation.

## Organic mulch

Crop residues and straw reduce soil evaporation roughly in proportion to how much of the surface
they cover; FAO-56 suggests about a 5 % reduction in soil evaporation for every 10 % of the surface
covered. The saving is largest while the crop is young.

## Plastic mulch

Plastic film nearly eliminates evaporation from the covered soil. FAO-56 notes that for vegetables
under drip irrigation with plastic mulch, crop coefficients are typically 10 to 30 % lower than
without it, with about 15 % as an average. Plastic mulch also warms the soil and controls weeds, but
has to be removed and disposed of properly.

## Other benefits

Mulch keeps soil moisture steadier between irrigations, reduces crusting and erosion, moderates soil
temperature and, for organic mulch, adds organic matter over time.
