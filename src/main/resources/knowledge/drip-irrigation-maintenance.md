## Why drip systems lose performance

Drip emitters have very small passages, so they clog from sand and silt (physical), from algae and
bacterial slime (biological) and from mineral scale such as calcium carbonate or iron deposits
(chemical). Clogging is gradual and uneven: some plants get less water than others while the
average flow still looks normal. Damaged laterals, rodent damage and loose fittings add leaks.

## Filtration and filter cleaning

Filters are the first defence. Screen and disc filters are cleaned when the pressure drop across
them rises: a differential of roughly 0.3 to 0.5 bar above the clean value is a common trigger.
Sand media filters are backwashed on a similar basis. A filter that needs cleaning much more often
than usual points to a change in water quality at the source.

## Flushing and chemical treatment

Flush mains, submains and the ends of laterals regularly, until the water runs clear, to remove
sediment that settles in the pipes. Biological clogging is controlled with chlorination and
mineral scale with acid injection that lowers the water's pH; both must follow the emitter and
chemical suppliers' guidance and local safety rules, and must not be mixed.

## Checking uniformity

Measure the flow of a sample of emitters across the block (for example at the start, middle and end
of several laterals) into containers for a fixed time. A well-maintained drip system delivers
closely matching flows: distribution uniformity around 90 % is typical when new, and a value much
below 80 % signals clogging, pressure problems or leaks that need attention.

## Pressure

Emitters are rated at a working pressure. Low pressure at the ends of laterals reduces flow there;
pressure-compensating emitters tolerate variation within their range. Record the pressure at the
head and at the far end of a block after commissioning, and compare against it at each inspection.
A sudden pressure drop with higher flow suggests a burst or open line; higher pressure with lower
flow suggests clogging or a closed valve.
