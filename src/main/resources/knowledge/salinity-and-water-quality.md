## Why salts accumulate

All irrigation water carries dissolved salts. Crops take up the water and leave most of the salt
behind, so salts accumulate in the root zone unless some water drains below it. Salinity is measured
as electrical conductivity: ECw for the irrigation water and ECe for the soil (saturation extract).

## Effect on crops

As soil salinity rises, crops must work harder to extract water, so they behave as if the soil were
drier, and some are also harmed by specific ions such as sodium, chloride or boron. Crops differ
widely in tolerance: beans and many fruit trees are sensitive, tomato and maize are moderately
sensitive, wheat is moderately tolerant and barley and cotton are tolerant.

## Leaching requirement

The leaching requirement is the extra fraction of water applied to wash salts below the root zone.
FAO Paper 29 estimates it as LR = ECw ÷ (5 × ECe − ECw), where ECe is the soil salinity the crop can
tolerate without significant yield loss. Leaching only works where drainage is adequate; otherwise
the water table rises and brings salts back up.

## Drip irrigation and salts

Under drip irrigation salts move to the edge of the wetted zone. A light rain can wash those salts
back into the root zone, so irrigating after the first light rains of the season, or before them, is
sometimes advised in saline conditions. Test source water at least once a year, and after any change
of source.
