## How solar pumps behave

A solar pump's output follows the sun: flow is lowest early and late in the day and highest around
midday, and drops under cloud. Irrigation is best scheduled within the productive solar window
(roughly mid-morning to mid-afternoon), with a storage tank or reservoir as a buffer where the crop
needs water at other times. Dust on panels can cut output noticeably, so panels should be cleaned
regularly.

## Grid power, tariffs and load shedding

Where grid power is interrupted on a schedule, irrigation should be planned inside the hours power
is reliably available, with long irrigations split across windows. Where time-of-use tariffs apply,
shifting pumping to off-peak hours lowers cost without changing the water applied.

## Measuring pumping efficiency

Energy per cubic metre (kWh/m³) is the simplest measure of how efficiently water is being pumped. It
depends on the head (lift plus pipe friction plus the pressure the system needs) and the pump's
efficiency. Comparing it against the pump's own healthy baseline shows wear; comparing it across
pumps shows where upgrades pay back first. Oversized pumps throttled by a partly closed valve waste
energy; so do undersized pipes with high friction losses.
